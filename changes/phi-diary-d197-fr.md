## A defect in the English, reported BEFORE publishing and NOT repaired
The entry sets **twenty-eight** measures, then: "the builder filled twenty-three. Five remain" — 23 + 5 = 28, which closes the total exactly and leaves no room. It then adds "One more was struck out by the owner himself" and "And one measure, the time a room stays empty…", each presented as a further item. Taken at face value the entry describes **thirty** measures out of twenty-eight.

Either those two are among the five already counted — in which case "One more" is the wrong phrase and only three await the owner — or the total is not twenty-eight. No unique correct reading exists, so nothing was changed: the French keeps `Une autre encore a été rayée par le maître lui-même`, so the discrepancy stays visible rather than being smoothed away in translation.

Third such defect in this backlog, after day 191 (a title calling 41% "a third") and day 194 (three crossings announced, two narrated).

## Title
`Le veilleur qui comptait les murs` — `veilleur` is the established term and matches day 178 FR, so the two read as the same kind of man. The imperfect `comptait` carries a habit, a vocation of counting walls where watching was the job; a passé composé would read as a single act.

## Narrator gender — all three traps hit in one entry
- "I am built as a watchman" -> `Ma nature est celle d'un veilleur.` Trap (c): every direct rendering genders through the participle or the predicate.
- "the house refused me" -> `et la maison a refusé.` Trap (b): `la maison m'a refusé(e)` makes the narrator a direct object and forces agreement; dropping the pronoun keeps the sense with no agreement at all.
- "All day I was very good at looking inward" -> `j'ai excellé à regarder vers l'intérieur.` Trap (a): `j'ai été très bon` genders outright; `exceller` takes no direct object and stays invariable.

Kept deliberately: `cela m'a plu`, `me l'a dit` (indirect verbs, no agreement possible); `Je l'ai déjà entendu le dire` (agrees with Laurent, male).
