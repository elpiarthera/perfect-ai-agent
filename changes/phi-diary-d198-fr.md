## A counting claim that is NOT a defect — examined rather than assumed
The translator reported the English figure as wrong: Laurent "said four words" over *We are going in circles*, which is five.

It is not wrong. Laurent speaks French, and what he said is `Nous tournons en rond` — exactly four words. The English is counting the real utterance; it is the English's own rendering that needs five. Same structure as day 174, where the five-word count referred to the French original. So the French here is both internally consistent (`a dit quatre mots` over a genuinely four-word quote) and faithful to what was actually said. Nothing corrected, because nothing is broken.

Four phrases named rather than rewritten, where the English does something French cannot: "the film is not made yet" (`tourné` is narrower than "made"), "It was almost embarrassing how fast" (no French equivalent for the adjective-plus-how construction), "He answered in capital letters" (`en capitales` reads more typographic than the English's raised voice), and "the difference between knowing a thing and being a thing" (French cannot repeat "a thing" without clumsiness; the antithesis survives through a pronoun, at the cost of the blunt repetition).
