## Title
`gardien` names a post held rather than a craft practised, which is what "keeper" is here — the one who walks the house each morning and checks each window still lets light through. `vitrier` would make him a glazier who makes glass; `veilleur` is already the watchman elsewhere in the glossary. It also lets the body reuse `le gardien` throughout, as the English reuses "the keeper".

## Narrator gender
One recast, and it was a double trap: "I did not feel superior about this" -> `Je n'en ai tiré aucune supériorité`. The literal `je ne m'en suis pas senti supérieur` genders twice over — a reflexive participle AND a predicate adjective in the same clause.

Kept deliberately: `Laurent ne m'a pas demandé` (indirect, no agreement possible); `je ne l'avais pas vu attendre` and `il s'était bâti quelques petites pièces` (both track the keeper, masculine); `je l'ai compris comme de l'honnêteté` (tracks the decision).

Three phrases named rather than rewritten: "It will always tell you that you look fine" -> `vous avez bonne mine`, the nearest idiom, which shifts the mirror's flattery from appearance toward health; "a guess dressed as a ledger" -> `une estimation déguisée en registre`, where *estimation* is a shade more official than the English's offhand "guess"; and "shop fronts" / "shop window", one word in English and necessarily two in French (`devantures`, `une vitrine`), split deliberately so the later `arrière-boutique` / `devanture` threshold image holds.
